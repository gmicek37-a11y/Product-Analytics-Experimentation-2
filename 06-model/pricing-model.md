## Recommendation

_**TO:** FinWise Leadership
**FROM:** Product
**RE:** Pricing Model Recommendation
**DATE:** August 22, 2026

**The problem**

We're at $10M ARR but leaking on both ends of the funnel: 2% of trial users convert to paid, and 60% of those who do convert churn within a year. Paid acquisition — where most of our marketing budget goes — is no longer producing meaningful growth, because we're spending harder to fill a bucket that's still leaking from the bottom.

**Growth-stage assessment**

We are in the Value Creation stage, not Revenue Expansion or Optimization. We've proven product-market fit exists, but we haven't yet proven the product reliably *delivers and sustains* value for the customers who try it. Our data — low conversion, high churn, engagement metrics (session duration, feature usage) declining over the customer lifecycle rather than holding steady — points to a value-realization gap, not a pricing or packaging gap.

**Recommendation: keep our current subscription model. Do not introduce freemium, transaction-based, or advertising-based pricing at this time.**

Subscription is the right match for how FinWise delivers value: cash flow visibility and financial management are recurring needs, used weekly, not one-off transactions. Our reverse-trial model already gives users full access to prove that value before asking for payment — a stronger mechanism for B2B than a permanently free tier would be. Switching models now wouldn't fix the underlying problem; it would relocate it. A freemium tier converting at 2-5% doesn't solve a 2% conversion problem, it just makes it permanent instead of time-boxed. Advertising requires the scale and engagement we don't have, and directly conflicts with the trust required to handle a small business's financial data.

**Supporting data signal**

Our conversion and churn numbers move together: 2% trial conversion tells us most users never reach a moment where the product's value is obvious. 60% one-year churn among the customers who *do* convert tells us the value that got them to pay isn't sustaining. Both point to the same root cause — value isn't landing consistently or durably — which no pricing model change can fix. Pricing optimization only pays off once there's a stable, retained base to optimize around; today, that base is still forming.

**Bottom line:** hold pricing model and packaging decisions until activation and retention improve to defined thresholds. Every dollar and hour spent redesigning monetization right now is better spent closing the value gap itself — that's the unlock, and it's the one that also makes any future pricing work land on solid ground instead of trying to fix growth with the wrong lever.

## Model signal

_Primary signal: one-year retention rate, trending up from the 40% baseline

This is the one metric that actually validates or invalidates the subscription bet, because subscription's core tradeoff (identified last turn) is that its revenue model only works if customers stick around. If retention doesn't move, no pricing model decision matters — you're optimizing packaging on top of a business that can't retain a customer either way.

Caveat that has to travel with this signal: we already established the current 60% churn figure is unverified — no clean cohort denominator, pooled monthly cohorts. You can't actually read this success signal correctly until that's fixed. So the real primary signal is two-staged: (1) get a properly cohort-indexed, denominator-clean retention number first, (2) then track whether it improves as activation and retention work (the Progress Bar, and whatever retention mechanic follows) takes effect.

Supporting signals (tell you why retention is or isn't moving)
Revenue per customer stability or growth, not just Revenue itself — since we found Revenue = Paid × a fixed number, watch whether that fixed number moves over time as you add tiers, add-ons, or usage-based components. A flat per-customer revenue figure for another 13 months would suggest the pricing structure itself isn't capturing more value even as the product matures.
Segment-level retention split, specifically checking the seasonal-need-fit hypothesis: if a meaningful segment of customers show usage concentrated in specific months (tax season) and churn right after, that's a signal subscription needs a variant (e.g., seasonal or usage-based pricing for that segment) rather than one-size-fits-all monthly billing.
Conversion-stage friction attributable to commitment, e.g., exit-survey or churned-trial-user feedback citing price/commitment concerns specifically — this would be the signal that the "asks for commitment before the relationship is proven" tradeoff is real and costing conversions, not just theoretical.
